3D-printed wind turbine

A 3D-printed wind turbine designed to score well on three things at once: low weight, high tower stiffness, and as much power as possible. We designed it in SolidWorks, checked the tower with finite element analysis, and tested it in a wind tunnel.

Team
Mahlet Copeland, Grace Qian, Muhammad Putra, Wen Cao, Jesus Martinez Martinez
Height and weight
16 in, 241.2 g
Stiffness
8.77 N/mm
Peak power
0.217 W at about 25 mph
Volume
16.76 in³, 99% of the limit

Constraints

The motor had to sit no higher than 16 inches, the whole turbine could not exceed 17 cubic inches of material, the base had to fit within 6 inches, blades could be at most 3 inches long, and no printed part could be larger than 9 inches on any side. That last rule meant the tower had to be printed in at least two pieces.

Blades

We chose three blades, since research showed that odd counts are more stable and that more blades give diminishing returns. Each of us sketched a profile, and we settled on a 10° angle of attack, within the recommended 10–15° range, and a 17.5° twist, based on studies of high-performing turbines.

In SolidWorks we lofted between the two profiles, patterned three blades evenly around the hub, and filleted every edge so they would print cleanly.

Tower

Some of our early designs used thin trusses, but we picked a tapered hollow cylinder because two solid halves are far easier to glue reliably than many thin members. The halves key into each other with a raised lip and a matching recess, giving plenty of glue area.

Our first version was over the volume limit. Instead of shrinking the diameter and losing stiffness, we cut oval openings through the walls, which brought the volume down to 16.76 cubic inches while keeping a wide, sturdy base.

Finite element analysis

We simulated a 9.8 N load on the motor housing, matching the physical test where weights pull on an eyebolt there. The model predicted 0.897 mm of deflection, about 11 N/mm of stiffness, and a minimum factor of safety above 1, with the highest stress around the upper slot.

Testing

On test day we measured wind speed with an anemometer, blade speed with an optical tachometer, and power, voltage, and current from the generator circuit. With roughly 25 mph of wind, power peaked at 0.217 W.

For stiffness, we hung weights from the housing in steps up to 6.3 kg, where deflection reached 7.05 mm and the readout stopped being reliable. The tower behaved linearly throughout, with a measured stiffness of 8.77 N/mm, somewhat below the FEA prediction.

What we learned

The tower was the strong point: light, stiff, and well within the rules. Power was the clear area to improve, and better blade aerodynamics would be the first place we would look next time.
